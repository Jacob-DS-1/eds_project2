Name: Yuhui Duan

---

Project 2 provided a valuable opportunity to explore how environmental data science can be used in climate change studies. Our team concentrated on the projected temperature changes in Manchester beyond the year 2050, analyzing seasonal variations, comparing regional trends, and identifying the primary drivers behind the warming. Also, this project enhanced my understanding of the role machine learning plays in environmental modeling.

In my view, our group formulated a focused and well-defined research question. We went beyond merely forecasting future temperatures by investigating warming patterns after 2050, examining seasonal fluctuations, evaluating Manchester's temperature changes relative to the surrounding area, and determining which factors were most influential for our predictions.

The presentation slides featured numerous informative visuals, including graphs depicting annual warming trends, seasonal shifts, feature importance diagrams, and spatial warming maps. These illustrations made it easier for the audience to grasp our findings and follow our reasoning more effectively.

Collaboration within our team was generally effective. Each member took charge of different sections, and eventually, we merged all components into a cohesive presentation.

Since I was not primarily responsible for the coding tasks, my main takeaway was gaining insight into how the methodologies were applied rather than constructing all the models myself. This experience taught me how to analyze climate datasets, how machine learning can enhance prediction accuracy, how to use visual aids to clarify results, how to communicate scientific work clearly, and how to collaborate productively in a group setting.

After observing other groups’ presentations, I noticed some compared a wider range of models, displaying strong technical skills. Nonetheless, I believe our group excelled in maintaining a clear research focus and delivering our results in an organized and accessible manner.
