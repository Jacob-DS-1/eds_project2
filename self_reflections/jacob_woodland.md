Project 2 was an extremely valuable learning opportunity for me around reproducibile research and workflows. Learning to use GitHub, and understanding conventions for creating repositories, has been one of the most useful parts of my Master's degree to-date and I am confident this will be a skill that I will use many times in the future. In Project 1, my understanding of the role and functionality of Git and GitHub was quite limitied and we largely only used it as a form of file storage. In contrast, in this project our group experimented much more with features such as `.gitignore`, `environment.yml`, `LICENSE`, branches and merges, and Git commands such as `status`, `add`, `commit`, `clone`, `merge`, `checkout`, `push`, `pull`.

One of our key project decisions was regarding our model selection and development approach. We choose to compare a non-linear approach, specifically `XGBoost`, against baselines - linear regression and Ridge regression, the latter allowing us to test a regularised linear approach. On reflection, we could have also tested a wider variety of non-linear machine learning models such as `LightGBM` and `random forest`, however we took the intentional decision to focus more on robust cross-validation, feature importance, ablation testing, and the fundamental climate significance of our results, rather than testing many models indiscriminantly.

A challenge for me in Project 2 was specifying a research question that was distinct enough from the machine learning task itself whilst still being complementary. I think we managed this well, however, by first ensuring that our model was giving valid projections, before then exploring the scenario, seasonal, spatial, and Manchester-specific variation in those projections, and finally offering potential physical interpretations for those trends that were grounded in environmental science. This allowed group members to bring their specific expertise to the project, with me focusing more on the machine learning aspects and my colleagues focusing more on interpreting the results and the underlying physical mechanisms.

Overall, I enjoyed Project 2 and developed valuable experience of conducting Earth and envrionmental data science in a reproducible way, with consideration for GitHub conventions, and within a multi-disciplinary team.
